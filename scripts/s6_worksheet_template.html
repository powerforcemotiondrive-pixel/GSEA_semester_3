📌 Lesson Summary & Operational Context

Closing an audit finding or incident investigation is not merely an administrative exercise—it is the statutory mechanism that prevents repeat catastrophic safety occurrences on the airfield. Under Regulation (EU) No 139/2014 Part-ADR.OR.D.005, when civil aviation authorities identify safety deficiencies, the aerodrome operator must formally demonstrate root-cause remediation, establish immediate interim risk mitigations (such as NOTAMs or operational restrictions), and agree on auditable verification milestones acceptable to the competent authority. In this capstone session, GSEA students examine an authentic EASA CAP dossier covering runway rubber hydro-blasting and jet blast deflector repairs, master formulaic commitment language (we formally undertake to, subject to authority concurrence, in the interim), simulate high-stakes regulatory negotiations with state inspectors, and dedicate extended workshop time to preparing their 30-minute interactive EASA extract presentations for Session 7.

Target Level: CEFR B1–B2 • Track: Technical English (AT • LV125E) • Time: 2 Hours (Teacher-Paced)

📋 Regulatory Briefing: EASA Part-ADR.OR.D.005 Framework

Under Regulation (EU) No 139/2014 Part-ADR.OR.D.005, receiving an audit finding notice triggers strict legal deadlines. Aerodrome operators cannot merely repair surface symptoms. They must isolate the underlying organizational root cause, determine whether flight safety is immediately hazarded (requiring provisional mitigations within 24 to 48 hours), and submit a formal Corrective Action Plan (CAP) detailing auditable engineering milestones acceptable to the competent civil aviation authority.

🔍 Opening Diagnostic: Audit Finding & CAP Principles

Entry Spot-Check

Question 01: Level 1 vs Level 2 Audit Findings

Under Part-ADR.OR.D.005, what distinguishes a Level 1 finding from a Level 2 finding?

✓ Model Answer: Level 1 poses an immediate hazard requiring action in 24–48h; Level 2 is systemic non-compliance requiring a 30-day CAP.

Question 02: Interim Risk Mitigations

What is an 'interim risk mitigation' and why is it mandatory before permanent works begin?

✓ Model Answer: A provisional operational safeguard (e.g., NOTAM, speed limits) to contain risk while permanent engineering works are executed.

Question 03: CFME Friction Threshold

What friction coefficient threshold triggers a mandatory Level 1 finding for runway rubber build-up?

✓ Model Answer: When measured friction decays below the minimum planning level (mu < 0.34 under EASA CS-ADR-DSN.B.015).

Question 04: Authority Concurrence

What does 'authority concurrence' mean in the EASA CAP approval process?

✓ Model Answer: Formal written acceptance by the national authority (DGAC/EASA) confirming that proposed milestones meet regulatory standards.

🗣️ Pair Discussion: Negotiating with Aviation Regulators

Point A • Realistic Milestones: Why do civil aviation inspectors reject CAP submissions with vague deadlines like “as soon as possible”? (Talking point: EASA standards require auditable target dates tied to physical verification).

Point B • Commercial Realities: How can an aerodrome manager convince an inspector to grant a 30-day timeline without compromising safety? (Talking point: By demonstrating that interim NOTAMs and speed restrictions fully mitigate operational risk).

🔍 Formulating SMART Corrective Milestones

• Specific: Define the engineering intervention clearly (ultra-high-pressure hydro-blasting at 2,200 bar, not runway cleaning).

• Measurable: Link completion to certified criteria (friction traces confirming mu >= 0.52, 45 kN pull-out torque).

• Achievable & Realistic: Schedule realistic nighttime closures without disrupting scheduled commercial traffic.

• Time-Bound: State the exact target date for on-site authority verification (28 October 2024).

Finding 041: Runway Friction Decay

Level 1 • CS-ADR-DSN.B.015

Survey Data: Continuous Friction Measuring Equipment (CFME) recorded average mu = 0.31 at 65 km/h with 1.0mm water film across the first 600m. Grooves 85% rubber-filled.

Interim Mitigation: NOTAM issued: “RWY 14L FIRST 600M BRAKING ACTION POOR WHEN WET. TAILWIND LANDINGS PROHIBITED EXCEEDING 5 KT.” Airlines apply +15% dispatch buffer.

Definitive Remediation: 4 nights of UHP hydro-blasting (2,200 bar) stripping 28,000 m² of rubber; 3 CFME calibration runs at 65 & 95 km/h. Target: 28 Oct 2024.

Finding 042: Blast Deflector Failure

Level 2 • CS-ADR-DSN.D.260

Defect Data: Holding Point P1 blast deflector shows severe anchor bolt corrosion. Three corrugated panels unseated; top FOD netting torn over 40-meter stretch.

Interim Mitigation: Service road Delta closed during widebody departures; ATC instructs all aircraft at P1 to maintain idle thrust until aligned with centerline.

Definitive Remediation: Bolting Grade 316 stainless anchors rated for 45 kN pull-out torque, new corrugated curved panels, and high-tension nylon FOD mesh. Target: 30 Nov 2024.

Part 4 Activity 4: Commitment Phrases for Regulatory Negotiation

Language Focus: When negotiating CAP milestones with civil aviation inspectors, aerodrome managers use precise formulaic commitment markers to balance legal compliance with operational reality.

📖 5 Formulaic Regulatory Commitment Phrases

Commitment Phrase Negotiation Function Authentic Airport Example Grammar Pattern
we formally undertake to Expresses a legally binding operational commitment to complete works “We formally undertake to complete hydro-blasting within 14 calendar days.” undertake to + [base verb] within [time]
in the interim, the operator shall Establishes an immediate provisional safeguard active before permanent works “In the interim, the aerodrome operator shall enforce a mandatory NOTAM.” In the interim, [subject] shall + [verb]
subject to authority concurrence Conditions an operational schedule upon formal civil aviation approval “Subject to your concurrence, our technical team will submit weekly photos.” Subject to [party's] concurrence, we will...
conditional upon physical verification Specifies that finding closure depends on auditable test proof “Finding closure is conditional upon physical verification of CFME traces.” conditional upon [noun/test result]
we respectfully submit that Introduces a polite, diplomatic pushback against excessive restrictions “We respectfully submit that closing the entire runway is disproportionate.” We respectfully submit that [clause]

✍️ 5 Practice Drills: Select the Correct Regulatory Phrase

1. The aerodrome operator ____________________ complete ultra-high-pressure rubber removal across the first 600 meters within 14 nights.

Options: [ formally undertakes to | respectfully submits that | in the interim ] • Answer: formally undertakes to

2. ____________________, air traffic control shall restrict all aircraft at Holding Point P1 to idle breakaway thrust.

Options: [ Subject to concurrence | In the interim | Conditional upon ] • Answer: In the interim

3. The revised 45-day blast fence reconstruction schedule is valid only ____________________ from the state oversight directorate.

Options: [ subject to authority concurrence | we respectfully submit that | in the interim ] • Answer: subject to authority concurrence

4. Formal Level 1 finding closure remains ____________________ on-site CFME calibration runs confirming mu >= 0.52.

Options: [ conditional upon physical verification of | we formally undertake to | in the interim ] • Answer: conditional upon physical verification of

5. ____________________ imposing a 30-day runway closure is commercially unviable given our verified nighttime hydro-blasting capacity.

Options: [ In the interim | We respectfully submit that | Subject to concurrence ] • Answer: We respectfully submit that

💡 Negotiation Tip: Balancing Assertiveness and Regulatory Respect

Avoid Confrontation: Never say: “Your deadline is impossible and will cost us too much money.”

→ Use Professional Diplomacy: “We respectfully submit that an immediate full closure is disproportionate given our interim NOTAM buffer, and we formally undertake to complete nightly hydro-blasting within fourteen calendar days.”

Part 5 Activity 5: Aerodrome Operator vs Civil Aviation Inspector Negotiation

Simulation Instructions: Work in pairs. Partner A acts as the Aerodrome Safety & Compliance Manager defending the proposed CAP schedule; Partner B acts as the Civil Aviation Inspector enforcing safety standards under Part-ADR.OR.D.005.

Role A: Aerodrome Safety Manager

Operator

Goal: Present proposed CAP milestones, justify why blast fence procurement requires 30 days due to specialized alloy delivery, and prove that interim NOTAMs fully contain flight safety risks.

Target Spoken Phrases:
• “We formally undertake to complete hydro-blasting within 14 calendar days.”
• “In the interim, the operator has published a NOTAM prohibiting tailwind landings.”
• “Subject to your concurrence, our team will submit weekly progress photos.”
• “We respectfully submit that closing the runway is commercially disproportionate.”

Role B: Civil Aviation Inspector

DGAC / EASA

Goal: Scrutinize the CAP timeline, demand verifiable proof, verify that Level 1 risks are mitigated within 24 hours, and grant conditional concurrence only with auditable progress checks.

Target Inspector Phrases:
• “The authority acknowledges receipt, but 30 days for Level 1 friction is excessive.”
• “We require physical CFME traces showing mu >= 0.52 before the NOTAM can be cancelled.”
• “Our concurrence is conditional upon certified torque pull-out test reports for all 40 bolts.”
• “What specific contingency is in place if the hydro-blasting vehicle suffers downtime?”

📋 Civil Aviation Inspector Peer Evaluator Checklist

Peer Evaluation

Partner B evaluates Partner A's compliance negotiation against these 4 statutory criteria before giving 1 minute of oral coaching:

1. Finding Classification: Accurately cited Level 1 immediate vs Level 2 30-day CAP?
2. Commitment Phrases: Accurately used undertake to, in the interim, subject to?
3. Realistic Milestones: Proposed SMART auditable dates & enforceable NOTAM buffers?
4. Assertive Diplomacy: Maintained professional composure without aggressive pushback?

Inspector (Evaluator): “Your interim NOTAM strategy for Runway 14L was very thorough. However, when proposing the 30-day blast fence timeline, remember to explicitly use formulaic commitment phrases like: we formally undertake to submit weekly progress photos subject to authority concurrence.”

Safety Manager (Operator): “Thank you for that observation, Inspector. We formally undertake to provide certified torque pull-out test reports every seven days, conditional upon your on-site verification.”

🎙️ Structure of a Professional Regulatory Negotiation

• Opening Acknowledgment: Formally acknowledge receipt of the finding reference number and classification.
• Immediate Containment: State the interim operational restriction already active (NOTAM, speed caps).
• Definitive Technical Fix: Explain the root-cause remediation with specialized contractor equipment.
• Binding Commitment: Conclude with a formulaic deadline commitment subject to authority verification.
