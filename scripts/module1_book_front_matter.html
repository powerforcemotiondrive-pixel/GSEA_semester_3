PEDAGOGICAL CODE & MINDSET

How to Succeed in English This Semester

Essential guidelines for continuous progress, formative evaluations, and successful semester validation.

🎯 Our Priority: Steady & Continuous Spoken Improvement

This communicative programme is specifically designed to build your spoken confidence, technical vocabulary, and professional communication skills as future civil aviation technical officers. There is no high-stress written exam. Your success depends on active weekly peer interaction, continuous effort, and steady spoken progress.

📈 1. Continuous Progress

Building gradually on the English competencies you already have, with regular practice in pairs and trios every week.

🗣️ 2. Formative Evaluation

Session 7 flipped lesson in teams of 3–4: teach an authentic EASA extract to peers and receive personalized feedback.

📊 3. Active Portfolio

Logging your reflections in the digital Learning Portfolio so you and your teachers can track your growth.

⚠️ Mandatory Portfolio Rule: Detailed Notes (Never One-Word Answers!)

Your digital Learning Portfolio is inspected and verified regularly by the Course Coordinator (Phil PARRIS). Entries must be substantive and reflective:

❌ Banned: One-word answers
Never write vague words like "good", "fine", "ok", "nothing", or "ras". These give your teachers zero information to assist you.
✅ Required: Detailed reflections
Explain what went well, which technical terms were challenging, and what action you will take before next session to improve.

The 6 Core Spoken Competencies (ICAO / MTF Standard)

1. Spoken Fluency: Natural tempo without long hesitations.
2. Interaction: Turn-taking, asking questions, active listening.
3. Vocabulary: Exact aerodrome & technical terms.
4. Grammar Control: Modal precision & cause-effect linking.
5. Pronunciation: Intelligible sounds & natural rhythm.
6. Comprehension: Understanding briefings & instructions.

💡 Teacher Autonomy & Class Pacing

You do NOT need to finish every written exercise in class! The worksheets are rich and comprehensive so you never run out of speaking material. Your teacher determines the pace and discussion depth based on spoken interaction.

Module 1 Core Competencies (AT • LV125E)

By the end of Semester 3 Module 1, GSEA technical officers will be able to demonstrate the following 6 operational capabilities:

01 EASA Regulatory Navigation & Scanning Skills

Competency: Skim and scan official European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) regulatory documents, Certification Specifications (CS-ADR-DSN), and Guidance Material (GM) to rapidly locate operational rules, dimensional constraints, and aerodrome compliance standards.
Evidence: Rapid scanning of EASA clauses, extracting notice periods, obstacle height limits, and threshold distances.

02 Modal Verb Precision & Legal Decoding

Competency: Accurately decode and vocally differentiate between mandatory legal requirements (shall, must), recommended safety guidance (should), and discretionary options (may), explaining operational consequences to aerodrome stakeholders in plain B2 English.
Evidence: Clear verbal distinction between strict law vs. best practice recommendations during aerodrome operational discussions.

03 Safety Alerts & Spoken Ramp Briefings

Competency: Digest dense EASA Safety Information Bulletins (SIBs) and aerodrome safety notices to extract critical hazards (apron vehicle congestion, speed limits, wingtip clearance, FOD) and deliver structured, punchy 90-second spoken briefings to ground personnel.
Evidence: Concise spoken delivery, clear prioritization of immediate hazards, professional tone without reading from notes.

04 Aerodrome Audits & Non-Compliance Reporting

Competency: Review authentic aerodrome safety audit reports, classify safety findings into Level 1 (immediate safety hazard) and Level 2 (non-immediate compliance defect), and justify severity levels using past deduction structures (must have, could have).
Evidence: Accurate categorization of audit findings, confident 2-minute verbal briefing to the Airport Director.

05 Incident Investigation & Multi-Source Synthesis

Competency: Cross-reference multiple disparate technical evidence sources (ATC radio transcripts, METAR observations, driver/pilot statements) to identify contributing human and organizational root causes and deliver a structured spoken debrief.
Evidence: Logical chronological synthesis, accurate cause-and-effect connectors, balanced presentation of multi-source findings.

06 Corrective Action Plans & Peer Teaching Synthesis (FE1)

Competency: Formulate clear, time-bound Corrective Action Plans (CAP) using formal commitment language (we undertake to, subject to approval), and collaborate in teams of 3–4 to deliver an interactive 30-minute flipped lesson teaching an EASA extract to peers.
Evidence: Structured 30-min interactive session, teaching 3–5 technical words, leading communicative exercises, and integrating teacher feedback.

SESSION 7 • FLIPPED LESSON SPECIFICATION

Formative Evaluation 1: Guidelines & Criteria

Detailed instructions on how to prepare and lead your 30-minute peer lesson in Session 7.

👥 Session Format: 30-Minute Team Presentation (Teams of 3–4)

Your team takes the floor to deliver an engaging, interactive peer lesson based on an official EASA documentation extract of your choice. You act as the facilitators: teaching the regulation, introducing specialized technical words, and leading your classmates through communicative exercises. The final 30 minutes of class are dedicated to whole-class feedback and teacher debrief.

🎯 Target 1: Plain English Regulatory Breakdown

Explain what the EASA regulation is about in clear, natural B2 English. Avoid reading long text directly from slides. Summarize the operational purpose and safety impact.

🔤 Target 2: Teach 3 to 5 New Technical Terms

Select key technical terms from your extract. Provide plain definitions, correct pronunciation, word stress, and practical aerodrome usage examples for your classmates.

💡 Target 3: Lead Creative Classmate Exercises

Engage the room! Create reading comprehension questions, a mini-quiz, a matching drill, or a pair speaking role-play. Keep classmates actively participating.

🤝 Target 4: Balanced Participation & Debrief

Ensure equal speaking time across all team members. Listen actively to classmates' answers and engage with your teacher's feedback during the class debrief.

Recommended 30-Minute Lesson Timeline

00–08 min: Introduction
Context, safety hazard, and plain English summary of the EASA extract.
08–16 min: Vocabulary Focus
Teach 3–5 specialized terms with definitions, pronunciation, and examples.
16–30 min: Interactive Practice
Classroom reading check, mini-quiz, pair role-play & student answers.

Module 1 Essential Vocabulary & Connecting Words

Core linguistic toolkits for aerodrome compliance discussions, modal precision, and technical briefings.

1. EASA Legal Modals

• Shall / Must: Mandatory legal requirement.
"The aerodrome operator shall establish a safety management system."
• Should: Recommended best practice.
"Vehicle drivers should undergo annual refresher training."
• May: Discretionary authorization.
"The competent authority may grant temporary exemptions."

2. Past Deduction Structures

• Must have + V3: Almost certain logical conclusion.
"The driver must have overlooked the stop bar marking."
• Could have + V3: Possible alternative explanation.
"The towbar pin could have failed under excessive torque."
• Cannot have + V3: Logically impossible event.
"The tug cannot have entered the runway with red stop lights on."

3. Conditional & Exemption Linkers

• Provided that: Strict condition attached.
"Works may proceed provided that wingtip clearance is maintained."
• In lieu of: Authorized substitute measure.
"Visual marshals were used in lieu of unserviceable taxiway lights."
• Subject to approval: Conditional upon authority sign-off.
"The safety case remains valid subject to regular audit review."

4. CAP Formal Commitments

• We undertake to: Formal corporate commitment.
"We undertake to repaint all runway holding positions by Dec 15."
• Has been commissioned: Formal implementation.
"An external audit has been commissioned to review FOD sweeping."
• Pending verification: Awaiting compliance check.
"The stand remains restricted pending friction testing."

💡 Additional Speaking Practice Prompt

Quick-Fire Pair Challenge: Choose one finding from Session 4 (e.g. deteriorated paint markings or vehicle speeding on Apron 2). Take 1 minute to explain: (1) what happened using a past deduction (must have...), (2) what the EASA rule requires (shall...), and (3) what corrective action your team undertakes to implement by next Friday.
