1. Executive Summary & Professional Aviation Relevance

Reading, explaining, and facilitating discussions around official European regulatory documentation is central to the role of civil aviation technical officers (TSEEAC), aerodrome inspectors, and operational managers. Whether briefing airport staff on a new EASA safety directive, guiding ground crews through ramp speed rules, or explaining audit findings, the ability to translate formal regulatory prose into clear, plain B2 English with accurate technical vocabulary transforms passive knowledge into active communicative leadership.

In this session, student groups lead 30-minute interactive workshops, presenting their chosen EASA extract, teaching key vocabulary, and engaging classmates with creative exercises. Imagination and creativity are strongly encouraged! At the end of the student presentations, your teacher will spend the final minutes of class providing constructive feedback and offering spoken language corrections.

2. Four Core Spoken Communicative Objectives (CEFR B2 Level)

OBJ 01 Spoken Breakdown of the EASA Extract

Explain the regulatory domain, core safety issue, and operational meaning in clear, plain English without reading from slides or notes.

OBJ 02 Teach 3 to 5 New Technical Words

Teach key specialized terms from your extract with simple plain English definitions and practical aerodrome examples.

OBJ 03 Lead Creative Classmate Exercises

Engage classmates with reading comprehension questions, mini-quizzes, or pair speaking tasks. Creativity and imagination are encouraged!

OBJ 04 Equal Contribution & Teacher Feedback

Contribute equally across team members and engage actively with teacher feedback and language corrections during the class debrief.

PART 1 & 2: ACTIVITY GUIDELINES • 30-MINUTE FLOW SECTION: EASA EXTRACT PRESENTATION

Part 1: Activity Guidelines: Leading Your 30-Minute Interactive Session

Review the 4 core steps for leading your 30-minute interactive session with your classmates based on your selected EASA documentation extract:

Step 1

Spoken Breakdown of the EASA Extract

Present your chosen EASA extract clearly. Explain what regulatory domain it covers, what the main safety requirement is, and why it matters in real aerodrome operations. Do not read aloud from slides or notes!

Step 2

Teach 3 to 5 New Technical Words

Highlight 3 to 5 key specialized terms from your extract. Explain what each word means in plain English and give a clear operational example so your classmates can understand and use it immediately.

Step 3

Lead Interactive Classmate Exercises

Distribute or project little exercises for your classmates to complete (reading comprehension questions, a quick true/false quiz, definition matching, or pair discussion). Creativity and imagination are strongly encouraged!

Step 4

Classroom Wrap-Up & Teacher Feedback

Check answers with the class, answer questions, and summarize the key safety takeaway. In the final minutes of class, the teacher delivers constructive feedback and spoken language corrections.

PART 3: PRESENTATION TOOLKIT • NATURAL SPOKEN DRILLS SECTION: SPOKEN PRESENTATION FLUENCY

Spoken Presentation Toolkit — Useful Phrases for Presenters

Deploy these practical, natural B2 phrases during your 30-minute delivery to present your extract, teach words, set up exercises, and debrief:

📢 Introducing the Extract & Context

  • “Good morning everyone. Today our team is looking at an extract from EASA Part-ADR regarding...”
  • “This regulation specifically applies to aerodrome operators and deals with...”
  • “The main purpose of this statutory requirement is to ensure that...”
  • “In practical terms, this means that airfield operations must...”

💡 Teaching New Technical Words

  • “The first key term we want to teach you today is...”
  • “In simple words, this term means...”
  • “For example, when an aircraft lands on a wet runway, this word describes...”
  • “Can anyone give another example using this word in context?”

✏️ Setting Up Classmate Exercises

  • “Now we would like you to look at the short reading exercise on the screen / handout.”
  • “Please work in pairs with the person next to you to answer questions 1 to 3.”
  • “You will have a few minutes to read the paragraph and find the two safety hazards.”
  • “Take a look at the quiz questions and decide whether each statement is True or False.”

🎯 Reviewing Answers & Handover

  • “Let us check the answers together. Table 2, what did you find for Question 1?”
  • “Does anyone have a different answer or interpretation?”
  • “That is correct, because under the EASA rule...”
  • “Thank you everyone for your active participation! We now hand over to our teacher.”

Language Clinic: Natural Presentation & Instruction Drills

Practice transforming stiff or hesitant presentation talk into confident, plain B2 English:

Scenario 1 (Contextual Hook):
❌ “Our paper is about rules for airports. It is a bit boring and long.”
✅ Plain B2: “Today our team is presenting an extract from EASA SIB 2024-09 on apron safety. This regulation is crucial because ramp vehicle speeding causes over 50 ground collisions every year.”
Scenario 2 (Defining New Technical Terms):
❌ “The word is 'displaced threshold'. It is in the dictionary.”
✅ Plain B2: “Our first new term is 'displaced threshold'. That means a runway threshold that is not located at the physical beginning of the runway, usually because of temporary construction work.”
Scenario 3 (Setting Up a Classmate Reading Task):
❌ “Read this and answer the questions if you want.”
✅ Plain B2: “Please look at Exhibit A on your sheet. With your partner, read the short incident notice and answer the two comprehension questions. You have four minutes.”
Scenario 4 (Checking Classmate Answers):
❌ “Does someone have the answer? Anyone?”
✅ Plain B2: “Let us see what you found. Table 1, what did your pair conclude: is this non-compliance a Level 1 hazard or a Level 2 finding?”
Scenario 5 (Concluding Your Presentation):
❌ “That is all. We are finished.”
✅ Plain B2: “To conclude, this EASA rule reminds us that clear reporting prevents repeat incidents. Thank you for your active participation, and we now hand over to our teacher.”

PART 4: OFFICIAL FE1 MARKING RUBRIC • PERFORMANCE BANDS REF: EASA-FE1-RUBRIC-20PTS

Official 20-Point Formative Evaluation Marking Rubric

Total: 20 Points • 4 Criteria (5 Pts Each)
Assessment Criterion Pts Teacher & Peer Expectations (CEFR B2 • Spoken Technical English)
1. EASA Extract Breakdown & Reading Comprehension 5 Clear, well-structured spoken breakdown explaining the regulatory extract and its practical aerodrome purpose in plain B2 English. Zero reading aloud from slides or notes!
2. Teaching Technical Vocabulary & Definitions 5 Effective teaching of 3 to 5 new technical words from the extract with accurate plain English definitions and concrete operational examples.
3. Classmate Exercises & Creative Engagement 5 Engaging, creative exercises (reading comprehension questions, quiz, matching, or pair discussion) that get classmates actively thinking and talking.
4. Spoken English Fluency, Pronunciation & Team Balance 5 Confident spoken English, intelligible pronunciation, natural conversational tone, and equal speaking contribution across all group members.

Four CEFR B2 Performance Bands

Exemplary 18–20 Pts

Dynamic student presentation; crystal-clear breakdown; excellent vocabulary teaching; highly engaging and creative classmate activity; equal team speaking.

Solid B2 14–17 Pts

Good explanation of the extract; clear vocabulary definitions; classmates participate actively in the exercise; minor language slips; good teamwork.

Developing 10–13 Pts

Overly lecture-based; exercises are rushed or unclear; unequal speaking time among group members; occasional reliance on written notes.

Needs Growth <10 Pts

Direct reading aloud from notes or slides; no interactive exercise for classmates; extract not explained clearly; lack of preparation.

💡 Session 7 Golden Rules (FE1)

1. Zero Reading Aloud: Speak directly and naturally to classmates. Reading from slides or notes is strictly penalized.
2. Get Classmates Active: Engage peers with reading comprehension, quizzes, or speaking tasks. Creativity is rewarded!
3. Teacher Final Feedback: Listen actively to your teacher's feedback and spoken language corrections to set portfolio goals.

ACTIVITY 5: SAFETY CHECK • REGULATORY COMMUNICATION SECTION: AERODROME SAFETY OVERSIGHT

Activity 5: Simple Safety Check: Clear Communication in Airfield Regulations

Review the 4 questions in the table below to analyze how reading comprehension, technical vocabulary, and plain English explanation protect operational safety on the airfield:

Simple Safety Question Agreed Operational Answer & Takeaway
1. What is the communication challenge? EASA regulations and technical aerodrome specifications use dense legal English that can be difficult for operational teams to interpret quickly during frontline airfield operations.
2. What could happen if misunderstood? Misinterpreting safety requirements leads to non-compliant airfield operations, inadequate risk mitigation, and heightened danger of runway excursions or apron vehicle collisions.
3. How do we fix it? Civil aviation technical officers must know how to break down complex texts into plain, actionable English, teach key vocabulary, and verify team understanding through interactive discussion.
4. Operational Takeaway Clear spoken communication and interactive peer engagement turn regulatory rules into everyday safe operational habits across international airport environments.
