Session 3: Special Requests & Runway Safety Cases

Article 71 Exemption Cases, Displaced Thresholds & Airline Operational Briefings

📌 Lesson Summary & Operational Context

Unplanned structural defects beneath active runways—such as stormwater culvert subsidence—cannot be repaired without altering certified runway dimensions. Completely shutting down an international runway disrupts global flight networks and costs airlines millions of euros. Instead, airside managers use Article 71 flexibility exemptions under Regulation (EU) 2018/1139 to maintain safe flight operations by establishing temporary displaced thresholds, reduced Runway End Safety Areas (RESA), and strict compensatory limits (dry-runway only, 15-knot crosswind caps, and dedicated RFFS forward standby). In this session, GSEA students examine an authentic runway exemption dossier (EASA/ADR-EXEMP/2024-009), master 5 professional connecting words (provided that, due to, in order to, in lieu of, unless), and deliver a structured 3-minute spoken briefing to airline flight operations managers with peer feedback.

Target Level: CEFR B1–B2 Audience: GSEA Student Training (TSEEAC Airside & Airport Ops) Total Duration: 120 Minutes

💬 Pair Discussion: Operational Questions & Talking Points

Speaking Warm-Up

01. Why can't an airport simply close a runway for 6 weeks to repair a drainage ditch?

💡 Talking Points: A total shutdown would divert hundreds of international flights, disrupt airline networks, cause severe passenger delays, and cost millions of euros. Major airports must find safe ways to carry out essential repairs while keeping runways open.

02. If the runway end safety area (RESA) is reduced from 90m to 55m, what extra rules can keep aircraft safe?

💡 Talking Points: When a physical safety buffer is reduced, we introduce strict operational restrictions: allowing landings only when the runway is completely dry, capping crosswinds at 15 knots, displacing the landing threshold forward, and stationing a fire engine nearby.

03. How would you explain a temporary runway modification to an anxious airline pilot?

💡 Talking Points: Be clear, direct, and reassuring. State the exact available landing distance (LDA), explain the extra safety measures in place, and use clear connecting words like "Operations are authorized provided that the runway is dry."

🎯 Aerodrome Rules Entry Spot-Check (Baseline Verification)

Knowledge Check
Question 01

What is an Article 71 exemption under EASA rules?

Plain English: A legal safety rule in European aviation law that allows an airport to temporarily adapt certain standards during urgent repairs, provided that compensatory safety measures maintain equivalent safety.

Question 02

What happens when a runway threshold is temporarily displaced?

Plain English: The designated landing point is moved further down the runway, reducing Landing Distance Available (LDA). The full runway length remains available for departures (TORA) in the opposite direction.

Question 03

What does RESA stand for, and what is its purpose?

Plain English: Runway End Safety Area: a cleared, flat buffer zone beyond the runway end designed to protect aircraft from damage in case they land short (undershoot) or overrun the end.

Question 04

Who must receive advance notification before temporary runway works commence?

Plain English: Airlines, flight operations dispatchers, air traffic controllers, and pilots must receive advance notice via official NOTAMs and AIP Supplements before any runway changes take effect.

OPERATIONAL CONTEXT: Heavy winter runoff caused acute soil erosion and structural subsidence around the reinforced concrete stormwater culvert located beneath the Runway 26 threshold safety strip. REVISED RUNWAY GEOMETRY: - Certified Total Runway Length: 3,000 metres. - Threshold 26 Displaced: Temporarily displaced forward by 180 metres. - Landing Distance Available (LDA): Reduced to 2,820 metres (sufficient for Code C/D commercial transports). - Take-Off Run Available (TORA): Preserved at 3,000 metres for Runway 26 departures. - Runway End Safety Area (RESA): Temporarily reduced from certified 90m to 55m adjacent to the excavation zone. 3. MANDATORY COMPENSATORY SAFETY MEASURES: In accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/1139 Article 71, continuous flight operations are conditionally approved subject to strict compensatory risk mitigation: (a) Dry Runway Constraint: Runway 26 landing operations are strictly restricted to completely dry surface conditions. (b) Maximum Crosswind Limitation: The maximum allowable crosswind component for landing aircraft is capped at 15 knots. (d) Dedicated RFFS Standby: One Major Foam Tender (RFFS Category 7) shall maintain a 60-second forward standby position at Taxiway B holding point during all landing windows.

📘 Key Connecting Words for Safety Cases & Conditional Rules

Connecting Word Plain English Meaning Real Airport Example Memory & Spoken Tip
Provided that / As long as On the condition that "Aircraft may land provided that the runway surface is dry." Introduces a strict mandatory safety condition.
Due to / Because of For the reason that "The safety area is reduced due to urgent culvert repairs." Followed by a noun phrase explaining the cause.
In order to For the purpose of "We displaced Threshold 26 in order to protect stopping distance." Followed by an infinitive verb ('in order to protect').
In lieu of In place of / Instead of "We positioned temporary markers in lieu of standard paint." Useful when one safety measure replaces another.
Unless Except if "No aircraft may land unless the crosswind is under 15 knots." Sets a clear boundary condition or operational cutoff.

✏️ Practice Drills: Choose the Correct Connecting Word

1. Aircraft may land on Runway 26                      the runway surface is completely dry.
Options: [provided that | in lieu of | due to] → provided that ('Provided that' introduces the mandatory safety condition: dry runway).
2. The available safety area was temporarily reduced                      urgent culvert reconstruction.
Options: [due to | in order to | unless] → due to ('Due to' is followed by the noun phrase 'urgent culvert reconstruction', explaining the cause).
3. The threshold was displaced by 180 metres                      give pilots full stopping distance.
Options: [in order to | provided that | in lieu of] → in order to ('In order to' expresses purpose and is followed by the base verb 'give').
4. High-intensity flashing guard lights were installed                      standard painted markings.
Options: [in lieu of | unless | due to] → in lieu of ('In lieu of' means 'instead of' or 'replacing').
5. Approaching aircraft must divert to Runway 14/32                      the crosswind component is 15 knots or less.
Options: [unless | in order to | because of] → unless ('Unless' means 'except if the crosswind is 15 knots or less').

💡 Executive Speaking Clinic: Linking Complex Rules Aloud

In operational safety meetings, avoid trailing, disorganized sentences. Use the professional pattern: "Due to [cause], we have [action taken] in order to [safety objective], provided that [strict condition] is respected." Practice saying: "Due to culvert subsidence, we displaced Threshold 26 by 180 metres in order to protect stopping distance, provided that the runway is dry."

Your objective is to explain the civil engineering necessity and prove that the 4 compensatory safety rules guarantee 100% safety.

Presenter
